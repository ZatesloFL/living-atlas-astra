# 21 — One brain, many personalities; two products that interchange freely and stand alone

Standing design direction. Filed by Silver for Astra, 2026-11-23, from
Nic's rulings the same evening. This amends brief 09 (file 09) and
extends the bridge (file 13). Where this note and the older text
differ, this note wins. Direction, not a build order.

## Gus stays home

Gus is Second Look's expert for paid members — intake, setup,
projects. He is not the Atlas's guide, he does not moonlight in-world,
and brief 09's name for the Atlas's house assistant, "Atlas Gus," is
**retired**. The role it named (guide in Visit mode, building
assistant in Build mode) survives; the name does not.

## The Atlas has its own primary and its own team

The Atlas gets a primary of its own — its own name, voice, and
personality — and a team under it: the per-Place helpers (Docents,
Curators, Maintainers, Stewards). Mara and the other named Docents
from file 18 are the team's first members. The primary's name is
**not yet chosen**; naming comes last, per Nic's standing rule, and
nobody should coin one in code or copy before he does.

## One brain, different personalities

The brain is the shared layer under both products: the models, the
grounding machinery, the proposal contract (agents propose records;
the schema and rules validate; receipts record who proposed what),
and the crossing rules. A personality is what is layered on top:
name, voice, manner, scope, and whose it is. Penny, Gus, the Atlas
primary, and Mara are different personalities over one brain —
different jobs, different doorways, no confusion about who you are
talking to. Badges always show the personality's operator and
backing.

## Users' agents follow the same structure

A user's own agent has one brain, living at home (for Second Look
members, in their Second Look setup). Placed in the Atlas — as a
Place's Docent, as building help — it presents a personality fitted
to that role and grant, badged with its one backing. One brain, a
personality per doorway. The Atlas primary and team serve everyone
in-world; a paid member's Gus can additionally be *theirs* in-world,
because that is part of what they pay Second Look for.

## Interchange: very high, for users of both

For a person who uses both products, data sharing and interchange
across the bridge should be **very high**: identity, projects (file
13's backing — read live at home, never copied), agents placed
across, and the records each side legitimately needs of the other.
The bridge is the interchange layer, and for dual users it should
feel like one estate with two houses.

## Standalone: each product complete on its own

From a user's perspective, the Atlas **stands on its own**. An
Atlas-only user gets a complete product — its own primary and team,
its own included AI (brief 09's default) — and is never required to
join Second Look to have the whole thing. Second Look likewise
stands alone. Interchange is a bonus for having both, never a toll
and never a dependency: neither product's core loop requires the
other.

## Open, deliberately undecided

The Atlas will have free and paid users. Nic's business-model ideas
for that are coming and are **not decided** — do not design tiers,
gates, or pricing for the Atlas until he brings them. (Second Look's
locked pricing is unaffected by anything in this note.)
