# 32 — Build experience, the live map, and one contextual intelligence

Standing direction from the owner, from his own device session in the live build,
2026-11-25 late evening. His overall verdict on the current build: "very good
overall. amazing work." What follows is refinement direction, not correction of
the model. It amends file 31's surface presentation and file 25's Build surface;
the contracts underneath (files 21, 26, 30, 31) are unchanged.

## 1. The Atlas Map is live

The map (the room graph, per the 2026-11-25 naming ruling) must always reflect
the world as it is now: places and things created, moved, or published appear
and change on the map as it happens. A map rendered from a stale snapshot is a
defect, not a view. (Owner evidence: an exhibit hall built in the same session
did not appear on the map.)

## 2. Build mode is one working surface, entered by words or a template

Build mode is re-founded on how the owner actually works:

- **Entry is a request or a template.** The person types what they want, or
  selects a template. There is no blank-canvas form first.
- **The working view is a sketch.** What is being made shows on screen in a
  sketch / light / soft mode — an edit mode, visibly not the full render. The
  person works against the sketch.
- **The surface is a sequence, and it is live.** The steps (the existing tabs'
  jobs) work in order, and while the screen stays on, doing things interacts
  with what is on screen — an action with no visible effect on the sketch is a
  broken step.
- **Save, then publish through the chain the destination implies.** The person
  can keep working or save at any point. Publishing moves the work through the
  approval chain for where they want to put it — the destination determines the
  chain (their own place, a public place, someone else's place each carry their
  own agreements), under the existing grant/crossing machinery.
- **Editing is branching.** Editing a published structure — your own or someone
  else's — branches it into this same build mode. The original stands untouched
  until the branch is saved and published through its chain. There is no
  separate editing surface.
- **Proposals must answer the request.** A proposal that does not correspond to
  what the person asked for reads as the Atlas ignoring them. Scripted seed
  proposals (file 25) must either track the request's plain meaning or be
  labelled as the samples they are — never presented as a response.

The audience preview is a step inside this sequence, not the surface itself and
not interleaved with the working state (an owner editing their own draft does
not meet "visitor cannot enter" as working copy).

## 3. One intelligence, contextual — no separate brands

"Atlas attending" and "Ask Atlas" as two named, separately-toggled surfaces are
too much machinery. There is one thing: **the intelligence on the network**,
present in context.

- Its surface is relevant to the page and the task at hand. In Build it is the
  building partner; at a door it speaks to the crossing; in a room it speaks to
  the room.
- It is not always "ask." It asks, tells, and suggests as the moment fits —
  labels name the action being offered (talk to Atlas, Atlas suggests), per the
  2026-11-25 lexicon ruling, never a persona and never a fixed brand stamped on
  every surface.
- The file 31 trust property survives as behaviour, not as a label: the
  intelligence is never silently present, and its initiative still runs only
  inside the holder's grant and envelope (file 31 §§3–4, landed as B1). What
  changes is the chrome: no second brand, and the presence signal is a single
  plain indicator — the Atlas is with you, or it is not (owner-confirmed
  2026-11-26: "we want an indicator when atlas is on and off, but then the
  rest needs a much simpler ui"). Everything else about the surface gets
  simpler.

## 4. Grants and approvals: contract proven, surface must lighten

The owner exercised the grant machinery on device and it worked end to end
(proposal → confirm → receipt). His verdict on the surface: "a big pain."
Direction for the experience pass, contract unchanged:

- Proposals speak plainly and briefly — what changes, for what, for how long —
  in the person's words, not in contract vocabulary.
- Receipts live in the record layer, one tap away. A full receipt identifier is
  never displayed inside a proposal card.
- Editing a grant or an approval is a conversation or a short list of the
  person's actual grants — never a re-run of the full proposal machinery for a
  small change.

## 5. Visitor movement: fly, zoom, and easier navigation

Owner-added 2026-11-26, after being walked through the current controls (walk
at fixed eye height; no fly, no zoom while visiting). Visiting gains:

- **Rise and fly.** A visitor can rise above the floor and move through the
  air inside a place they are in. Phone: a hold-to-rise control beside the
  walk stick, and a Land control that returns them to a standable spot below.
  Keyboard: E rises, Q lowers — the same language as Build's Fly camera. A
  place may set a ceiling; nobody rises out of the place.
- **Look closer (zoom).** Binoculars, not travel: the visitor stays where
  they stand and the view magnifies. Phone: two-finger pinch. Desktop: mouse
  wheel. Walking eases the view back to normal.
- **"See it up close" on a card.** A thing's card offers to bring the visitor
  to a good viewing spot in front of it, inside the same place — the direct
  answer to "I can see it but I can't get close."
- **"Back to the entrance."** Every place offers a plain way back to where
  the visitor came in, for anyone who is lost. No map skill assumed.

Guardrail, binding on all of it: movement is viewpoint inside a place already
entered. Flying and zooming never cross a door, never enter a place, and never
defeat the Lens — what a visitor's Lens withholds is withheld from every
height and every magnification. Crossings remain deliberate door acts.
