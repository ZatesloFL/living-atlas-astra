# 31 — Ask the Atlas: the agent is the interface

Standing direction. Nic's rulings, 2026-11-25, made from inside the live build: he uploaded a photo, added a video, and then had to fight forms to do anything with them — and ruled that the forms are the wrong surface. This brief names what replaces them.

## 1. What the house intelligence is called

The Atlas's house intelligence is addressed as **the Atlas itself** — the user *asks the Atlas*. No character name, no mascot, no bot branding. The world attends.

- The lead-agent role filed as "the Primary" (files 21, 25) keeps that name in design documents. In user-facing copy, the user meets the Atlas.
- Per-Place helpers keep their own names (Mara, Ellis, and the rest) — helpers are characters *in* the world. The Atlas is the world.
- "Gus" is Second Look's alone and never names anything here.
- If a proper name for the role is ever chosen, it is chosen last and by Nic's ruling only. Nobody coins one in code or copy.
- Lexicon: **ask the Atlas** / **the Atlas** (attending) is the canonical way to refer to this role in UI copy, lane reports, and briefs.

## 2. The interface law

The user should basically never get forms. Setup and operation of the world are things the user **asks the Atlas to do, in words** — chat or voice — and the Atlas does them and explains as it goes:

- connect a source, propose and approve a mapping
- read content and propose its declaration (both dials, every dimension)
- place a thing in a room; build and tune a place
- set Lens positions, door terms, standing preferences
- appoint helpers and stewards; grant, lease, revoke

The Atlas is the user's own house agent for this world — their executive assistant to it — not a help bubble bolted onto a settings page.

## 3. How it acts

File 21's contract governs every act: **agents propose, schema and rules validate, receipts record who proposed and who confirmed.**

- The Atlas acts for a user under that user's own helper grant to it — scoped, leased or standing, revocable, receipted, like any helper grant. Hard floors cannot be granted away.
- Nothing is declared in the user's name that the user did not confirm. The honesty rule binds conversation exactly as it binds forms: a proposal is labeled a proposal until the user confirms it; the Atlas never presents a default, an inference, or its own reading as the user's declared state.
- The working pattern already exists in the build: lane B's photo proposals read the content and propose the dials for the owner's confirmation. That pattern, spoken instead of formed, is this brief's whole mechanic.

## 4. The initiative envelope — tunable by the user

How much the Atlas speaks and does unasked is the user's setting, expressed in words, changeable at any time, settable per area of life in the world, and receipted when changed:

1. **Quiet** — the Atlas speaks when spoken to. Notifications, search, and the ordinary web-like UI. It acts only in the open, when asked.
2. **Conversational** — a chat / phone companion. It proposes, explains, asks, and acts on the user's yes.
3. **Proactive** — a chief of staff for the world: it advises, warns, and protects **within an envelope the user specifies** — what it may do unasked, what it must always ask about, and what it watches for (a soft-limit gap ahead of a crossing, a helper grant about to lapse, audience readings diverging from a declaration, a door whose terms changed).

Protective never means surveillance: the file 26 privacy map binds the Atlas exactly as it binds everyone — no visiting history, no private rooms, no Lens settings disclosed, to anyone, including the person the Atlas serves in any other role.

## 5. The attending layer

At the user's option, the Atlas attends **alongside** a visit as an extra active context layer: pre-flight in words, gaps named before a crossing, a place's Docent knowledge on tap, fetching and preparing while the user looks. It is visibly on or off, never silently present.

This is not the Lens. **The Lens filters; the Atlas attends.** The two words are never interchangeable in copy.

## 6. Forms retire to the audit layer

Existing forms and panels — Connected sources, declaration confirmation, Lens sliders, standing editors — remain as the **inspector / record surface**: where a user checks precisely what the Atlas did, corrects it by hand, or works manually because they prefer to. They are no longer the front door for setup.

New capability is built **conversation-first**. A form may follow as its record view; it never precedes as its only path.

## 7. Consequences and sequencing

- The design review (Duet's desk) gains a **conversation audit**: for each setup and operation task, the conversation that should exist, and every place today's forms force data entry an agent should have done.
- File 25's talk / place / tune proposals with the Primary are the seed of the general pattern, not a Build-mode extra.
- This brief is standing direction, not a build order. Build orders follow per surface, filed through Silver; consequential choices in them are Nic's rulings, as always.

## 8. What this is not

- Not a renaming of Gus, and not a persona.
- Not permission for the Atlas to act unasked beyond the posture the user set.
- Not a removal of manual control — the audit layer is a right, not a fallback apology.
- Not a change to any filed rule about grants, receipts, standing, or floors. It changes the surface those rules are operated through: words first.
