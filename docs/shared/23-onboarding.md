# 23 — Onboarding: "See your space"

Standing design direction. Filed by Silver for Astra, 2026-11-23,
from Nic's design the same evening. Builds on files 13, 20, 21, 22.

## The promise

Onboarding opens on **"See your space"** — not a platform tour, not
a blank plot. The first thing a new person gets is their own place,
taking shape.

## How it runs

1. **Questions, plus an offered search.** We ask some onboarding
   questions and we *offer* to search the internet about them —
   offered, never done silently. Whatever the search surfaces, the
   person **marks what is actually them**. Nothing unmarked is ever
   asserted as fact about them: the system proposes, the person
   declares. (The same honesty rule as Second Look intake.)
2. **The interview.** Hobbies and interests — and explicitly,
   **whether they want their local area included or not**. Locality
   is an opt-in, never a default: a place may be rooted in its
   owner's real geography or float free of it.
3. **The first rooms are built from what they gave us.**
   - **The "Google Drive" room** — the personal archive: their
     photos and things, organized for them. This is where connected
     sources (round 4) land; it is the home for a person's own
     stuff.
   - **The workshop** — **everyone gets one** (file 22: every
     sign-up spins up both accounts). For Second Look members it is
     their agent's in-world workshop: where the agent works, where
     they monitor it and use Second Look directly. For everyone, it
     is also **Second Look's front door inside the Atlas**: the
     workshop onboards them to a **free Second Look account** and
     explains the product and the pricing — staffed by **the real
     Penny and Gus**, Second Look's own personalities present as
     themselves. The workshop is Second Look's premises in-world
     (backed by Second Look, file 13's pattern), not an Atlas room
     with Atlas helpers imitating them.
4. **Three ways to start.** A starter place built from the
   interview; **from scratch**; or **from a template**. Their
   choice — none is the "real" one.
5. **Second Look members don't start cold.** What their agent
   already knows is proposed for the starter place through the data
   stream (file 21) — marked and confirmed by them like everything
   else, never silently carried over, never re-asked from zero.

## The reset law

**Starting over is only hard when other people are using your space
for their stuff.** For a private build, a reset loses only **shape
and projection** — the arrangement, the current rendering — and
never **data or capabilities**: things, photos, records, grants,
standing, and agent connections all survive a rebuild untouched.
The world is a projection of the records; you can always re-project.
The only legitimate friction in starting over is unwinding other
people's reliance on your place — real obligations to real people,
never retention tricks by the platform.
