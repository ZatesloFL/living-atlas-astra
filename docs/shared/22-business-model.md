# 22 — The business model: the Atlas is Second Look's loss leader

Standing direction. Filed by Silver for Astra, 2026-11-23, from Nic's
ruling the same evening. This **answers the open item in file 21**

## The model in one paragraph

The Atlas is a **loss leader for Second Look**. Second Look's revenue
supports it. It is a free add-on for Second Look users and a
standalone website in its own right, with Second Look baked in as the
way to interact with the world outside the Atlas. There are **no
separate users**: every sign-up gets both accounts (eventually — the
target state, phased in). The stream between the two products lives
in the **data**; on the front end, each product holds only
**references** to the other.

## What that means, piece by piece

- **One revenue engine.** Second Look sells the help — the expert
  setup, the maintained agent, Nic's time. The Atlas is the world
  that help is for: the showroom that never closes, where members'
  agents live in public and visitors watch what having one looks
  like. The Atlas does not need its own pricing to justify existing.
- **Free, and not gated on dollars.** No Atlas tier restricts a
  normal active user. A user with their own model API key — whose
  usage costs the platform nothing — never needs to pay and never
  feels restricted by price. The cost base supports this: the Atlas
  is cheap by design (data instead of assets, receipts instead of
  archives; the entire prototype's live AI spend to date is under
  half a cent).
- **Moderation is the Lens; reward is standing.** The Atlas rewards
  structurally good behavior through its mechanics — declared vs
  measured, the visible gap, receipts, standing that compounds —
  and lets each visitor's Lens do the moderating (file 20). There
  is no central taste police and no paywall doing a filter's job.
  **Standing is never sold**: paid anything buys capacity and help,
  never reputation, or the measurement system becomes advertising.
- **One user, two accounts, two front ends.** Underneath, one person
  with both accounts and a high-interchange data layer between them
  (file 21's bridge: read live at home, never copied). On the
  surface, the Atlas site and the Second Look app/site are separate
  front ends that reference each other — links and backing pointers,
  not embedding. From inside the Atlas, Second Look is how you reach
  the world outside it: your real agent, your projects, the outside
  services. From inside Second Look, the Atlas is where your agent
  goes to be in the world.
- **Standalone still holds.** File 21's rule stands: the Atlas is a
  complete experience on its own site, its own primary and team, its
  own included AI. "No separate users" describes the account model,
  not a dependency of the experience — the Atlas front end never
  requires a Second Look visit to work.

## In reserve, unbuilt

If a real cost ever demands Atlas-side revenue, the candidate seams
are additive capacity only — a larger included-brain allowance,
AI-generation allowance on the house brain, scale/storage, more of
the team's sustained time — never restrictions on visiting, the
Lens, basic building, helpers, or standing. They are a drawer that
may never open. Do not build tiers, gates, or pricing for the Atlas
unless Nic reopens this note.

## Separation

The two products are built as one estate. If they ever need to be
broken apart, that gets dealt with then; it is not a design
constraint now. Keeping the Atlas technically able to run lean on
its own (above) is the cheap insurance, already in place.
