## Performance on the Pixel

**Partial — unmeasured.** The target is stable 60 fps in both scenes and both orientations; there is no defensible measured Pixel estimate yet. The overlay shows actual elapsed-time fps and total render draw calls, including bloom. It starts hidden and is toggled with ◈.

The renderer batches repeated geometry, uses one static 1024px shadow map, applies four low-cost postprocess passes, and caps DPR at 1.65 (below the brief’s maximum of 2). Low frame rates trigger stepped resolution reduction down to 0.85. Camera movement is eased without bob/roll. Scene/lens rebuilds recreate the small world and its interaction gates; initial shader compilation, new sign textures, and scene transitions are the most likely sources of a first-use hitch.

The cloud’s software-rendered Chromium observed approximately 8–11 fps in the sampled checks, with roughly 21–28 draw calls from the entrance views; those are **not Pixel measurements** and are not a 60 fps pass. Other viewing angles and the first shadow render can produce higher draw-call counts. The 22 automated browser checks passed without JavaScript/shader errors; a separate fullscreen enter/exit check also passed. Eleven rule tests passed. No test was skipped or expected to fail in the final runs. The first extended browser run stopped on a case-sensitive source-label assertion (CSS renders that label uppercase); the assertion was corrected and the full 22-check run then passed.

## Design choices worth comparing

- A small dusk garden campus with an open timber studio, warm thresholds, tactile floor grain, cool canopy trees, and an editorial serif title. On-device fonts are used; every scene asset is generated in code.
- A mostly unobstructed central walk makes the Tallahassee doorway easy to find in portrait. A left-side workbench anchors the first view; room labels and preview prompts help with orientation.
- One central lens/crossing evaluator drives physical gates, visibility, cards, and map explanations. Door confirmation never turns a view-only window into an entry permission.
- Normal author filters can hide human-authored exhibits; Silver is kept independent and can only be dimmed.
- The private room is represented by an explicit minimal record, not a hidden loaded interior.
- The Build action is present and explains the tier sequence instead of presenting unfinished editing controls.

## Known problems

- Game-quality appearance, comfortable thumb placement, smooth camera feel, and sustained Pixel 60 fps are still awaiting Nic. Automated desktop/phone-viewport checks do not settle those outcomes.
- Hosted Pages deployment is unverified because the runtime network policy blocks the Pages hostname and GitHub API. Draft domain additions for `api.github.com` and `zateslofl.github.io` were saved without changing any prior custom entries. To enable hosted verification from this cloud environment, review and save those changes in environment settings, then publish the environment; saving the proposal alone has not applied or published it. Phone access uses the phone’s own network.
- Dynamic resolution only steps downward during a session; reload restores the initial cap. Its practical sharpness/performance tradeoff needs Pixel feedback.
- Scene rebuilding on lens changes is intentionally small but may hitch on the first shader/texture use. No Pixel trace has been captured.
- A few policy ambiguities remain recorded in lane/questions.md. In particular, multi-step soft exceedances become preview doors unless a hard limit blocks them, and the explicit Night Market example takes precedence over the Commons’ nominal zoning cap.
- Historical structures are stylized sample geometry, not architectural reconstructions. Sources are pointers to verify, not verified citations. Identity and ratings are explicitly simulated.
- All state is in memory and resets on reload. Tiers 2 and 3 are unimplemented, not partially working features.
