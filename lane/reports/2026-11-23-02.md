## Implementation and judgments

`interior-data.js` attaches `layout.interior`; `interior-kit.js` validates the
rectangular shell, frontage, arrivals, partitions and up to eight exhibit bays.
`interior-space.js` resolves the 3D entry/exit and arrival while retaining the
canonical Place graph used by Page. `interior-world.js` renders the enclosure,
frontage and one atlas of mounted labels. This is a rectangular hall kit, not a
general floor-plan editor or queued Build mode.

The 18×24m hall is a separate scene, `hall:boycott`, to keep the Quarter's trees,
landmarks and sky out of the interior draw cost. It is spatially the existing
boycott Place, not a second Place with copied content or grants. The original
exhibit placement composition, fixtures, sample titles/warnings and readers are
reused. Each fixture can scale with Style while its bay center stays fixed.

The film and gallery occupy opposite front walls; listening and newspaper have
separate rear side bays; the bus sits at the back with space to approach and walk
behind it. Solid partitions prevent selecting exhibits through walls. A central
aisle and two cross aisles provide approach and stand-back space. Exhibit labels
are physical boards beside their pieces rather than DOM labels displaced across
the view.

The new physical door is projected into 3D pre-flight and the 3D Atlas. Existing
Lens, grant, confirmation, cancellation and receipt machinery remains in charge.
Outside landings record the Quarter as the occupied context; entering commits
the boycott presence. Lost access ejects safely. Saved locations include their
space; legacy outdoor boycott positions resume at the clear frontage without
